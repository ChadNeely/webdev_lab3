A Weekend Trip to Grand Island

Last summer, my friend Luca and I drove up to Michigan for a weekend on Grand Island. We wanted a short adventure that would let us spend most of our time outside, so we packed our camping equipment, drove north, and planned to backpack, swim, and hike.

Getting There

The drive was part of the experience. We left with our packs, sleeping bags, food, water, and basic camping supplies. The farther north we traveled, the more the trip felt like an escape from our normal routine. We used the drive to talk about the route, check our supplies, and decide what we wanted to see first.

After arriving near the island, we organized our gear before starting out. Carrying everything on our backs meant that we had to pack carefully. We brought the things we needed most and left behind anything that would add weight without being useful.

Backpacking and Camping

Backpacking made the weekend feel different from an ordinary day trip. Luca and I had to pay attention to the trail, the weather, our energy, and the supplies we had packed. We took turns leading and checking the map so that neither of us had to handle every decision.

At camp, we set up our sleeping area, prepared food, and talked about the next day's plans. The quiet surroundings made it easy to notice details that are usually lost in a busy schedule, including wind through the trees, waves along the shore, and the changing light in the evening.

Swimming in Lake Superior

One of the best parts of the trip was swimming. The water was cold, but that made it feel refreshing after carrying our packs and hiking in the sun. We stayed close to shore, took our time getting used to the temperature, and dried off while enjoying the view.

The shoreline gave us a place to rest between activities. We watched the waves, explored the beach, and talked about how different the landscape looked compared with home.

Hiking the Island

Hiking gave us a chance to explore beyond the beach. The trail moved through wooded areas and toward viewpoints where we could see the water. Some sections were easy to walk, while others required more attention because of roots, uneven ground, and the weight of our packs.

We did not try to rush. The best moments were the breaks when we could look around, take pictures, and appreciate that we had made the drive for a simple weekend outdoors. The trip reminded me that a good plan should leave room for unexpected discoveries.

What I Remember Most

The most memorable part of the weekend was sharing the work with Luca. We drove there together, carried our gear together, and made decisions as a team. Backpacking, swimming, and hiking gave us a full weekend of activities without needing a complicated schedule.

Grand Island was a good reminder that an adventure does not have to be long to be meaningful. A planned route, reliable equipment, and a good friend were enough to create a trip that I would like to repeat.
